Opening Hook ~45 sec
Stand still. Let the room settle. Then open with the question — no preamble.
Here's a question worth sitting with: when business students graduate, what have they actually practiced?
They've read cases. They've written reports. They've analyzed decisions that someone else already made. But they have almost never made a consequential decision themselves — one where a wrong call costs them market share, kills their margins, or forces layoffs in the next round.
That gap between knowing and doing — that's the problem we're here to close.
The Problem ~40 sec
Business education is theoretically strong and experientially weak. Instructors know this. They run simulations — but the tools available are either expensive, static, or both. The leading platform, Capsim, is used in over a thousand institutions. It's good. But it tells you your score. It doesn't tell you why you lost.
Students get a ranking, not an education.
And none of these platforms treat sustainability as anything more than an optional add-on. In a world where ESG is a core business constraint, that's a structural failure in the curriculum.
Our Solution ~60 sec
Slow down here. This is the product. Let each point land.
B-Sim AI is a macroeconomic business simulation platform built for universities. Students run virtual companies. The companies compete inside a shared economy.
That word — shared — matters. When your team cuts prices to gain market share, every other team feels it. The market shifts. They have to respond. That's what real competitive pressure looks like. No simulation we know of does this at this level.
After every round, our AI doesn't just score the teams. It audits them.
It identifies the exact decision that failed — say, you overproduced in Q2 and sat on inventory — explains why that decision caused the outcome, and tells the team what to change before Q3. That's not feedback. That's coaching at scale.
And sustainability is embedded from day one. Not a checkbox. Not a penalty. A survival constraint. Teams that ignore ESG trade-offs pay for it three rounds later. Exactly as they would in a real company.

We use an existing LLM API — Anthropic or OpenAI — not a custom-trained model. The simulation engine calculates outcomes. Those outcomes, combined with the team's decision data, are passed as structured context to the LLM, which generates the qualitative feedback. ✓ Strategy

Frame this as a deliberate architectural choice, not a limitation. "We use proven API infrastructure to focus our build on what differentiates us: the simulation logic and decision structure."

T2
"How does the shared economy work technically? What ensures one team's decision actually affects others?"
Technical
+
Answer

All teams submit decisions before the round closes. The simulation engine then runs a single simultaneous calculation pass — not sequential — applying all decisions together to a shared market state. Price changes shift supply-demand curves for all participants. Hiring decisions affect labor market dynamics. The market state that each team sees at the start of the next round is the output of everyone's combined moves, not just their own.

G3
"What happens if two teams make the same decisions? Is the simulation still meaningful?"
ChainSection G
+
Answer

Even identical decisions produce different outcomes in a shared economy — because outcomes are determined by the aggregate of all teams' decisions, not each team's decisions in isolation. If two teams both choose to flood the market with low-price products, neither gains the market share they expected — they cancel each other out and compress margins for everyone. That's precisely how competitive markets work. Identical strategies don't produce identical results when the market is reactive. In fact, that's one of the most important lessons the simulation teaches: mimicking a competitor's strategy without accounting for what happens when they do the same thing is a strategic error.
